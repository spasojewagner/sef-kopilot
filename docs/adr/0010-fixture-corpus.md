# 10. The fixture corpus: official SEF examples kept byte for byte, generated mutations, recorded verdicts

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-04
- Deciders: Marko Spasojević
- Technical story: milestone M1, branch `feat/core-fixtures-corpus`

## Context and problem statement

The brief asks for a corpus of 200 or more documents for M1: CEN's EN 16931 test files,
Peppol's examples, the SEF examples the Ministry of Finance publishes, and synthetic mutations.
Later milestones use the same documents: the API's upload tests (M2), the repair loop (M3), the
golden sets of the evals (M6). Four questions had to be settled. Which documents may live in
the repository? How are invalid documents made, and how do they stay in step with the rules?
How does the corpus show a change in behaviour? And how is it kept the same on every platform?

## Decision drivers

- Licences: third-party artefacts are fetched and never modified (ADR-0005); OpenPeppol's
  repository ships no licence file, so this project never redistributes it.
- Never a real company's invoice; synthetic documents use invented parties and identifiers that
  only satisfy the check-digit algorithms (brief §7).
- An invalid fixture must say what is wrong with it and what must be reported, and that claim
  must be checked, or it rots.
- Any change in a verdict, from our rules, a profile or a rule-set upgrade, must be visible in
  review, without tests that break whenever a message is reworded.
- The same results on Linux, macOS and Windows checkouts.

## Considered options

1. **Commit the Ministry's examples byte for byte with a manifest; generate mutations from
   synthetic bases with a catalogue of text edits; record the pipeline's verdicts on the whole
   corpus as counts per rule, checked by the tests** — chosen.
2. Write each invalid fixture by hand.
3. Build the mutations inside the tests only, without files.
4. Snapshot whole reports: messages, lines and XPaths.
5. Copy CEN's and OpenPeppol's examples into `fixtures/` as well.

## Decision outcome

Option 1. Concretely:

- **Official SEF examples** (`fixtures/invoices/official/sef/`): the 17 documents of the
  Ministry's page of 11 July 2025, committed as published. Official materials of a state body
  are not copyright works (Law on Copyright and Related Rights, Article 6). `manifest.json`
  records the page, each file's URL, size and SHA-256, and a test checks every file. They have
  CRLF line endings and no final newline, so `.gitattributes` stores them unnormalised and the
  whitespace hooks skip them. CEN's and OpenPeppol's examples stay in `third_party/`.
- **Synthetic bases** (`fixtures/invoices/synthetic/`): hand-written, each with a comment that
  states its purpose; two new ones settle an advance with the Serbian extension, in dinars and
  in euro, valid under the SRB profile without a warning.
- **Mutations** (`fixtures/invoices/synthetic/mutations/`, 43): generated by
  `scripts/make_fixtures.py` (`make fixtures`) from a catalogue entry per file: a name that
  starts with the target rule, the base, the mistake in one sentence, text edits that must each
  match exactly once, and the rules the SRB profile must report, with multiplicity. The
  generator puts the mistake and the expected rules in the file's header. Mistakes are the kind
  an ERP or an integration makes (a PIB typo, the pre-2012 VAT rate, a date in the local format,
  a bare ampersand), every SRB-CIUS rule is the target of at least one, and most report exactly
  one rule. Tests check that each mutation is reported exactly as its header says and that the
  files are what the generator writes.
- **Verdicts** (`fixtures/invoices/verdicts.json`): for each of the 93 corpus documents and each
  profile that applies to it (`srb` and `en16931` for Serbian documents, `srb` for mutations,
  `en16931` for CEN's 19 examples, `peppol` for OpenPeppol's 10), validity and how often each
  rule was reported as fatal, as a warning or set aside; no lines, messages or XPaths. `make
  fixtures` rewrites it; one test per document compares.
- CEN's and OpenPeppol's unit tests (1 471 test documents in 339 files) remain conformance tests
  of the Schematron layer (ADR-0005). With them the corpus holds over 1 500 documents.

### What building the corpus showed

- With valid check digits for their placeholder PIBs, 15 of the Ministry's 17 examples are
  valid under SRB; under plain EN 16931, 9 are invalid, 7 of them only for what SRB-CIUS widens.
  Both statements are now tests.
- EN 16931's BR-CO-25 (a due date or payment terms when an amount is due) is not in CEN's UBL
  Schematron, so nothing checks it; whether SEF requires it is to be confirmed (M5).
- CEN's BR-53 looks for the VAT total in the accounting currency with `//cac:TaxTotal`, which
  also finds the RSD totals in the Serbian extension. On an invoice with the extension, only
  SRB-028 reports a missing RSD breakdown.

### Consequences

Good:

- A change in behaviour becomes a reviewable JSON diff across the whole corpus, including
  documents nobody wrote an expectation for.
- The mutations are realistic, labelled invalid documents ready for the CLI, the API tests, the
  repair loop and the eval golden sets.
- Byte-for-byte official files with pinned digests on every platform.

Bad / accepted:

- When behaviour changes on purpose, `make fixtures` must be run and the diff reviewed; the
  review is the cost and the point.
- Text edits depend on the bases' formatting; a base change breaks the edits loudly, naming the
  mutation and the text that no longer matches once.
- Counts per rule do not catch a changed message or location; the unit tests of each layer do.

## Pros and cons of the options

### Hand-written invalid fixtures

- Bad: they drift from the rules, and nobody can tell later what each was meant to show.

### Mutations only inside the tests

- Bad: nothing for the CLI, the API, the repair loop and the evals to use; nothing a reviewer
  can open.

### Snapshots of whole reports

- Bad: every reworded message or moved element breaks dozens of snapshots, and people learn to
  regenerate them without looking.

### Copying CEN's and OpenPeppol's examples

- Bad: OpenPeppol's repository grants no right to redistribute it, and copies would hide which
  version of the artefacts they came from.
